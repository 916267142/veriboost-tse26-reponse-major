\begin{generalcomment}
	This paper presents VeriBoost, 
	an approach to accelerating fault-tolerance verification in wide-area networks (WANs) by reducing the space of failure scenarios. 
	VeriBoost combines two techniques: Link Pruning and Link Compression, 
	and is integrated with SMT-based, simulation-based, and graph-based verifiers. 
	The evaluation examines 260 real-world WAN topologies and reports verification speedups on datasets with synthesized BGP and OSPF configurations.
\end{generalcomment}

\end{revresponse}

\begin{revcomment}
Please resolve the inconsistency between “at most k failures” and “exactly k failures.”

The verification objective and the SMT constraint concern at most k link failures, but several descriptions of simulation-based verification and Scenario Aggregating refer to selecting exactly k failed links. The example following Definition 1 similarly states that five symbolic links with $k=2$ produce C(5, 2)=10 concrete failure scenarios, whereas the stated definition includes
$\sum_{i=0}^{2}C(5,i)=16$ scenarios.

Checking that a property holds under all scenarios with exactly k link failures is insufficient to establish tolerance to at most k failures, 
particularly for intents such as waypointing, 
where additional failures may reroute traffic through the required waypoint. 
Scenarios with k-1, k-2, \dots, 1 failures must also be checked.
\end{revcomment}
\begin{revresponse}

\begin{revcomment}[breakable]

it would be interesting to discuss whether VeriBoost can provide additional benefits over existing DCN-specific network compression techniques, 
such as Bonsai and Origami.
